\section*{Question 2}
\subsection*{2 (a)}
Using the normalization $\mathbb{E}[M^*] = 1$, we find the stochastic discount factor $M_t^* = a - b'xR_t$ 
that satisfies $\mathbb{\hat{E}}[M^* \cdot xR] = 0$. In particular, we have:
\begin{align*}
    0 
    &= \mathbb{{E}}[M^* \cdot xR] \\
    &= \underbrace{\mathbb{{E}}[M^*]}_{\displaystyle = 1} \cdot \mathbb{E}[xR] + \text{Cov}(M^*, xR) \\
    &= \mathbb{E}[xR] - b'\text{Cov}(xR, xR) \\
    &\implies \mathbb{E}[xR] = b'\Sigma \\
    &\implies b = \Sigma^{-1}\mathbb{E}[xR] 
\end{align*}

$xR$ is a Tx10 vector of excess returns with T = 1,202 months in the sample from July 1926 to August 2026. Verifying that $\Sigma$ is invertible,
we find that:
\begin{align*}
    1 
    &= \mathbb{{E}}[M^*] \\
    &= \mathbb{E}[a - b'xR] \\
    &= a - b'\mathbb{E}[xR] \\
    &\implies a = 1 + b'\mathbb{E}[xR] \\
\end{align*}
Therefore, we are able to compute the stochastic discount factor $M_t^* = a - b'xR_t$ for each month in the sample and obtain the following scatterplot:

\section*{Question 3}
\subsection*{3 (a)}
Consider a representative investor with wealth $W_t$ in a two-period economy. Let
$R_{f,t}$ be a baseline asset. We solve the following optimization problem:
\begin{align*}
    \varpi_t^*
    &= \arg\max_{\varpi_t} \mathbb{E}_t\!\left[U(W_{t+1})\right], \\
    \text{s.t.}\quad
    W_{t+1}
    &= W_t \cdot R_{w,t+1}
     = W_t \cdot (R_{f,t+1}+\varpi_t' xR_{t+1}),
\end{align*}
where $xR_{t+1}$ is the vector of asset excess returns relative to $R_{f,t}$.

Using the power utility function
\[
    U(W)=\frac{W^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma},
\]
where $\gamma$ is CRRA, we rewrite the problem as
\[
    \varpi_t^*
    = \arg\max_{\varpi_t}
      \mathbb{E}_t\!\left[
        \frac{
          \left(W_t \cdot \left(R_{f,t+1}+\varpi_t' xR_{t+1}\right)\right)^{1-\gamma}
        }{1-\gamma}
      \right].
\]

Thus, the first-order condition of the maximization problem is
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{E}_t\!\left[
      \frac{
        (1-\gamma) [W_t \cdot
        \left(R_{f,t+1}+\varpi_t' xR_{t+1}\right)]^{-\gamma}
        \cdot xR_{t+1}
      }{1-\gamma}
    \right] &= 0 \\
    \implies
    \mathbb{E}_t\!\left[
      \left(R_{f,t+1}+\varpi_t' xR_{t+1}\right)^{-\gamma}
      \cdot xR_{t+1}
    \right] &= 0 \\
    \implies
    \mathbb{E}_t\!\left[
      R_{m,t+1}^{-\gamma} \cdot xR_{t+1}
    \right] &= 0.
\end{align*}
As a result, the SDF $M_t=R_{m,t+1}^{-\gamma}$ prices the vector of excess returns.

\subsection*{3 (b)}
The moment function is given by $\epsilon(\gamma) = R_{w,t+1}^{-\gamma} \cdot xR_{t+1}$. 
Its sample estimator is $g_{T}(\gamma) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \epsilon(\gamma)$.
Since there is 1 moment condition and 1 parameter, the GMM estimator $\hat{\gamma}$ is obtained by solving
the minimization problem $\hat{\gamma} = \arg\min_{\gamma} g_{T}(\gamma)'g_{T}(\gamma)$, where $W = I$.

\subsection*{3 (c)}
Following the same procedure in Question 2 part (e) in Problem Set 1, I estimate the out-of-sample
forecast of $xR_{e,t+1}$, $\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{OS}[xR_e]$, and construct a managed portfolio 
using $z_t = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{OS}[xR_e]/ \sigma_{e}^{2}$ as a trading signal based on the dividend-price ratio. 
Thus, given 1 parameter $\gamma$, we have 2 moment conditions:
\begin{align*}
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[
      R_{w,t}^{-\gamma} \cdot xR_{w,t}
    \right] &= 0, \\
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[
      R_{w,t}^{-\gamma} \cdot (z_{t-1} \cdot xR_{w,t})
    \right] &= 0.
\end{align*}

Using an identity weighting matrix, we have the following optimization problem:
\[
    \hat{\gamma} = \arg\min_{\gamma} g_{T}(\gamma)'g_{T}(\gamma),
\]      
where $g_{T}(\gamma) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \epsilon(\gamma)$ and $\epsilon(\gamma) = \begin{bmatrix}
R_{w,t}^{-\gamma} \cdot xR_{w,t} \\
R_{w,t}^{-\gamma} \cdot (z_{t-1} \cdot xR_{w,t})
\end{bmatrix}$.

Solving for $\hat{\gamma}$ using the same sample of 973 months from December 1940 to December 2021, we obtain the following GMM estimate of risk aversion:
